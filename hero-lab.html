A — what is live now

A slow Ken Burns push on a still photograph, a gradient scrim, and the headline animating in letter by letter on load. It is clean, but the motion stops once the page has settled — after two seconds the hero is a static image.

B — depth

The photograph and the type track the cursor at different rates and opposite directions, so a flat image reads as though it has depth. Scrolling also pushes the image in while the type lifts and fades at its own pace. This is the effect doing most of the work on the expensive-looking sites — it is parallax against pointer position, roughly fifteen lines, no library. Amplitude here is deliberately low; push it further and it tips into gimmick fast.

C — reveal

The image is uncovered by a rising mask rather than fading in, and each headline line rides up out of its own box a beat behind the one above. The push then continues for six seconds instead of stopping. This is the most editorial of the three and the closest to how a fashion or fragrance house opens a page. It costs nothing but timing — no dependency, no build step.

My read

C, with B's pointer parallax at low amplitude. The reveal gives the page an opening gesture, and the pointer depth keeps it alive afterwards instead of freezing into a photo. Both are pure CSS and a small amount of JavaScript — they would add roughly 2KB to the 11KB already there, with no React, no bundler and nothing to maintain.

One caution. This brand's whole argument is that it is unhurried — six-week cure, cold process, hand-wrapped. Aggressive motion fights that. The reason places like Aesop and Le Labo feel expensive is restraint executed precisely, not effect volume. Everything here is deliberately under-driven for that reason, and I would rather tune it up on your say-so than start loud.
